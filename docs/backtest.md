# Task D: backtest

Generated by `src/backtest/run.py` (selection rules in `src/backtest/strategies.py`, engine in
`src/backtest/engine.py`, frozen model signals in `src/backtest/signals.py`). Figure:
`docs/figures/21_backtest_equity.png`.

**Rules.** One rebalance per quarter. A portfolio uses only 13F filings public by the 45-day deadline and
trades at the close of the next trading day, so every filing it uses was public before the trade; late
filers wait a quarter. Long-only, equal-weight, 20 issuers, common stock and ADRs. Costs 10 and 20 bp per
side on traded weight. Clone strategies run from the 2017Q4 filings (Feb 2018); model strategies only
over the 2023+ test years with the Milestone 6 models frozen. Alphas: monthly, Newey-West t-stats (3 lags),
through 2026-08 where the factor data ends. Return series run to 2026-10-14.

## Conclusion

**Copying hedge funds from their 13Fs did not beat the S&P 500 here, and nothing earned a significant factor alpha.** Traded the day after the filing deadline at 10 bp per side, the consensus best-ideas portfolio returned 9.3% a year over 2018+ against 14.5% for SPY (Sharpe 0.40 vs 0.66), with a Fama-French 5 + momentum alpha of −1.8% (t = −0.7). On the 2021+ subperiod, where daily-price coverage is high, it fell further behind (5.1% vs 14.9%). The best style clone, the growth/tech cluster, roughly matched SPY with more risk (14.8%, Sharpe 0.53, max drawdown −55%). It had no factor alpha (+1.7%, t = 0.5): its returns are its market beta (1.18) and growth tilt. The multi-manager/quant cluster is the only clone with a t-stat near 2, and only against the equal-weight seed benchmark (+6.3%, t = 2.0). Against FF5 + momentum its alpha is −0.8% (t = −0.5): beating the equal-weight seed benchmark, which is tilted to small caps, is a factor exposure, not skill. The activist clone's 2021 spike is one position, GameStop, drifting to about half the book inside a single holding window, then sold at the next rebalance. On the 2023+ test years the frozen models split. C2's ALS new-buy list returned 24.1% vs SPY's 21.9% (Sharpe 0.96 vs 1.10; FF6 alpha +4.3%, t = 0.9) and C1's predicted adds returned 16.5% (alpha −6.8%, t = −1.2). The re-entry baseline, the best C2 *predictor* in Milestone 6, was the worst *portfolio* (4.5%): predicting what funds buy is not the same as predicting what goes up. With about 10 strategies and 40 test months, none of these differences is distinguishable from noise: most factor-alpha t-stats are below 1.5 in absolute value, and the only ones near 2 are negative (the event-driven clone in 2023+, the mostly-cash value cluster). Costs barely matter (20 bp instead of 10 lowers annual returns by at most 0.8 pp, for the re-entry list, which turns over most), and treating names every fund sold as −30% instead of 0% moves them by 0.0–0.9 pp. The honest finding is the standard one: after the 45-day disclosure lag, 13F cloning delivers market-like returns with style tilts, not alpha.

**Returns of unpriced names.** Stocks without Yahoo daily prices earn the filled quarterly return
(13F-implied or delisting return) spread evenly over the quarter's trading days. **Those returns run
quarter end to quarter end, while positions are held filing date to filing date, so the timing of the
unpriced names is approximate**: a position entered in mid-May earns two-thirds of the Q2 return and
a third of the Q3 return, not its actual May-to-August move. Spreading also smooths those names, which
biases volatility and betas down. Names with no return at all earn 0% (sensitivity below at -30% for
names every seed fund sold).
