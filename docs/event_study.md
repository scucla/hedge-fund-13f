# Copycat event study: is 13F news priced in when it becomes public?

Generated by `src/analysis/event_study.py` (definitions in its docstring). Figure:
`docs/figures/22_event_study_car.png`. Acceptance times: `src/analysis/edgar_times.py` (EDGAR filing headers).

## Answer

**Mostly yes: the market reacts a little on the day a 13F becomes public, and there is nothing left to earn by copying afterwards.** There is no run-up before disclosure ([−5,−1]: −0.1%, t = −0.7). On the first two trading days new positions beat SPY by a small amount. Fund-weighted that is +0.42% (t = 4.0); event-weighted +0.17% (t = 1.8), with a median of zero. So the typical new position doesn't move at all, and the average comes from a minority that do. After that the excess return fades. Days +2 to +10 add +0.2–0.3% (not significant), and days +11 to +60 lose 0.7% event-weighted (t = −1.4) and 1.25% fund-weighted (t = −2.2), so by day 60 the cumulative path is slightly below zero. A copycat who buys at the close after the filing, as the backtest does, misses that reaction. For after-close filings it is the next session's move; for intraday filings part of it happened before the filing. Buying at the next open might catch part of it, which daily closes can't measure. That matches the backtest, where cloning earned no factor alpha. The reaction is largest, fund-weighted, for activist funds (+1.0%, t = 2.6) and for funds outside any cluster (+0.6%, t = 3.2). Event-weighted, it is largest for healthcare (+0.8%, t = 2.6) and growth/tech (+0.3%, t = 2.7) filings. It is absent for event-driven and multi-manager funds, whose new positions are mostly deal trades and small slices. Three caveats. (1) 29% of events have no daily prices, and 60% of those stocks later left the exchange; the priced sample is mostly survivors, which if anything flatters the long window. (2) The 0.5%/99.5% winsorizing was added after seeing OTC price glitches. Unwinsorized, the event-weighted [+11,+60] mean turns positive (+1.1%) while its median stays at −0.7%, and the fund-weighted mean stays negative (−0.9%). (3) Four windows times several splits invite false positives; only the fund-weighted day-0/1 reaction is clearly significant.

**Events:** 290,724 new positions (common stock and ADRs) in 77 seed funds' 13F filings,
2015Q2-2026Q2. Abnormal return = stock minus SPY, summed over each window of trading days around day 0, the first
trading day the filing could be traded on.
