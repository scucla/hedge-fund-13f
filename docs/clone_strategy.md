## Conclusion

**More return than the S&P 500, but no significant alpha: the extra came from market beta and the winners' growth tilt.** Copying the top 5 positions of the ten seed funds with the best trailing three-year 13F returns, each bought the day after that fund's own filing, returned 27.5% a year from May 2023 to October 2026 at 10 bp per side (27.1% at 20 bp), against 21.9% for SPY. Its Sharpe ratio was the same as the index's (1.09 vs 1.10), with more volatility (19.8% vs 14.8%) and a beta of 1.16. The Fama-French five-factor plus momentum alpha is +1.5% a year with a t-stat of 0.3, and the alpha against SPY is +3.4% (t = 0.9). It beats the equal-weight benchmark of all seed holdings by 11% a year (t = 2.7), but that benchmark lost to SPY too: the gap is the large-cap growth exposure of the funds that happened to win, not stock-picking skill a copycat can harvest. It lands where the growth/tech cluster clone does (30.7%, Sharpe 1.03, alpha t = 0.5) and above the consensus and model portfolios. Most of the lead over SPY was built in the second half of 2025 and in 2026, so with 40 months of data it rests on one stretch for growth stocks. The rules were fixed on 2018–2022 and committed before this run (`5aed44c`), and the leakage audit, including a poison test of the selection, found nothing. One test period, one stretch, no alpha: past 13F winners kept riding their factor exposures, and the clone rode with them.
